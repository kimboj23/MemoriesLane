MemoryLane · capture service · team explainer

How a saved link becomes evidence

What happens between someone pressing “+ Lưu liên kết” and a sealed, checkable copy of a post: which tools do the work, which ideas we took from Atlos and Bellingcat’s auto-archiver, how we judge whether a capture really worked, and what it costs to run.

Step by step

The nine stations

  1. 1

    Save a link

    A team member presses “+ Lưu liên kết” on any page of the hub, or a community site forwards what a member of the public submitted. Community sites sign every request with their own key, which can only send, never read.

    • The link is cleaned: tracking parameters (fbclid, igsh, mibextid…) are dropped and the address is put in one standard form.
    • The same link saved twice is one item. It can be filed under any number of incidents, and it is captured once.
    • Share links (facebook.com/share/…, threads.com/share/…) are followed to the real post.
    Builturls.py · inbox_core.py · intake.pyidea: “save from anywhere” (raindrop.io, Zotero)Planned: phone share sheet, browser extension
  2. 2

    Queue

    Each save becomes a row in a Postgres table. Workers claim the next row with FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED, so several can run side by side without a separate queue server. Each worker runs two captures at a time.

    Builtworker.pyfrom Atlos: the job queue lives in the same Postgres (Oban), no Redis
  3. 3

    Choose the way out

    Meta refuses requests from our server’s datacenter address: Threads answers HTTP 429, and Instagram redirects to its login page. So Threads and Instagram go out through a residential connection in Vietnam (DataImpulse). Facebook and ordinary websites still work from the server and go direct, at no cost.

    • One capture, one IP. Each capture gets a random sticky port, so all its requests come from one Vietnamese home or mobile address, and the next capture gets another.
    • Only pages and data are proxied. Images, videos and scripts come from Meta’s file servers, which serve our server fine, so they go direct. That cut paid traffic by 4–10×.
    • The proxy password lives only in server settings. Captures record “proxy” or “direct”, nothing more.
    Builtegress.pydecided 21 Oct 2026
  4. 4

    Two witnesses capture at the same time

    Like two people independently recording the same scene, two tools capture each link in parallel. Either one can save the capture if the other fails.

    Media witness · yt-dlp

    • Downloads the post’s video, audio, photos, thumbnails and subtitles (Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, X).
    • Keeps a cleaned copy of the post’s metadata (author, date, counts). Never the raw .info.json, which can contain a login session.
    • Behind the proxy: reads the post through it, then downloads the files direct, and falls back to the proxy if refused.
    yt-dlp 2026.8.19, pinnedas in auto-archiver’s generic extractor

    View witness · Chromium

    • Opens the post in a real browser (Playwright, Chromium 153) with a Vietnamese language and timezone, in a virtual display rather than headless.
    • Saves a screenshot as served (login walls included, as evidence), a full-page screenshot with pop-ups removed, the HTML, the text and the page’s metadata.
    • Also captures the platform’s public embed (Facebook plugin, Threads /embed, Instagram /embed/captioned), which often shows a post a logged-out visitor can’t see.
    • Keeps the platform’s own restriction notice, such as Meta’s “legal request” text behind “See why”.
    • For social posts, also records everything the browser fetched during this same visit into a replayable archive (WACZ). “Mở bản lưu tương tác ↗” replays the post as captured, from the file alone, without contacting the platform.
    Playwright 1.63, pinnedembed capture: ours
    Builtwitnesses/media.py · witnesses/view.py · pipeline.pyBuilt: a replayable archive (WACZ) of the same browser visit, for social posts
  5. 5

    Judge: a verdict instead of “archived”

    The old platform marked a link “archived” even when it had only saved a login page. Now a quality gate reads what both witnesses brought back and gives one verdict. These are the labels the hub shows:

    VerdictHub labelMeans
    completeĐã lưu đầy đủAuthor, full text and the media or embed were captured.
    partialLưu được một phầnReal content, with something missing, e.g. text cut short without a login.
    walledBị chặn đăng nhậpOnly a login wall came back. A wall is never “complete”.
    restrictedBị hạn chế truy cậpThe platform withholds it from this region or viewer. Its notice is kept as evidence.
    unavailableĐã bị gỡ / không khả dụngThe platform says it’s gone. Logged out on Facebook this is uncertain, so it gives “walled” with a note to check while logged in.
    rate-limitedTạm bị giới hạn · sẽ thử lạiThe platform refused our server (HTTP 429). Not a result: retried after 30 min, 2 h, 6 h, 12 h, 12 h, holding the platform’s other links back too.
    failedLỗiNothing usable after all attempts.

    Wall, removal and restriction messages are matched in Vietnamese and English. A platform’s notice (“Không có thread”, a login prompt) is never taken as the post’s text.

  6. 6

    Seal and store

    Every file is hashed with SHA-256. A manifest lists every file, its hash, the tools and versions used, and the verdict. The manifest is hashed too, so one hash pins the whole capture:

    manifest sha256 · 4f2a9c…e81d (example)

    • Files are stored under their own hash, so a stored file can’t be changed without its name changing.
    • Capturing again adds a new version. Nothing is overwritten or deleted.

    Why this matters, in plain words

    A hash is a fingerprint for a file. The same file always gives the same fingerprint, and changing even one pixel or one comma gives a completely different one. The manifest is the capture’s packing list, and its fingerprint stands for the whole capture.

    • Proof nothing was altered. If someone says “you edited that screenshot”, anyone can recompute the fingerprint and compare it with the one recorded when it was captured.
    • One code covers everything. Quote the manifest’s fingerprint; any change to any file, or to the list, breaks it.
    • History stays honest. New captures and people’s corrections sit beside the sealed one, never inside it.

    It turns “trust us, this is what the post looked like” into “check it yourself”. The fingerprint alone proves a file hasn’t changed since it was sealed, not when it was sealed. That is what the trusted timestamps below add.

    Trusted timestamps: proof of when

    Minutes after a capture is sealed, its manifest fingerprint is sent to two independent timestamping authorities (FreeTSA and DigiCert, standard RFC 3161). Each signs “this fingerprint existed at this time”. They receive only the fingerprint, never the content.

    • The link’s page shows “⏱ Thời điểm đã được chứng thực” with both times, and each signed token can be downloaded.
    • Anyone can check a token offline with standard tools (openssl ts -verify), without trusting us or our server.
    • Two authorities, so one going away or being doubted doesn’t remove the proof.

    Off-site copy

    Every night the stored files and an encrypted database dump are copied to Backblaze B2, into the same bucket the old archive already used, under its own folder. The bucket uses Object Lock: nothing uploaded can be changed or deleted for 90 days, even by someone who takes over our server. The copy never deletes anything, and stored files are named by their fingerprint, so a changed file is refused, never overwritten.

  7. 7

    People check, and correct the machine

    Everything new lands in the Inbox. The whole team’s links show by default, filtered behind one “Bộ lọc” button. Captured pages are opened only in a locked-down viewer, because captured content can be hostile.

    • When the machine is wrong (say, “removed” for a post that only needs login), “Sửa thông tin” corrects the status from a dropdown, with a required reason, plus the title, author, post date and text.
    • The sealed capture is never touched. The correction sits beside it with who, when and why, and the page shows both: “Sửa tay bởi … ; máy ghi: …”.
    • Duplicate photos. Every photo gets a small visual fingerprint that survives resizing and recompression. A link or a community submission shows “Ảnh tương tự đã có trong kho” when the same picture is already in the archive, among what the person may see.
    Builtitem corrections, loggedphash.py (difference hash, Pillow only)idea: Atlos and auto-archiver’s perceptual hashes
  8. 8

    Organise into projects and incidents

    Projects group incidents by theme. An incident has attributes (status, date, location, tags, custom fields), source material, and a feed of changes and comments. Type @reviewer or @name to bring people in.

    • Five roles per project: Owner, Manager, Editor, Viewer, Data-only viewer. Someone outside a project gets “not found”, never “forbidden”.
    • Data-only viewers never see names, comments or the feed.
    Builtworkspace.py · access.py · people.pyfrom Atlos: its design (projects, incidents, roles, update feed), not its code (GPLv3)
  9. 9

    Publish to the community sites

    Nothing leaves the hub until someone entitled (a manager, or a member with publication access) approves a public version. One approval is enough, and “Xin ý kiến thứ hai” asks for a second opinion when unsure.

    • Locations are blurred by policy (a private home is never exact), and photos are stripped of GPS and camera data.
    • Published records go into a signed public feed. The community map and Hồ sơ Nhân quyền check the signature before showing anything, so nobody can fake a record. A withdrawal leaves a tombstone, and the sites remove the record.
    Builtpublish.py · geo.py · derive.py · feed.pyPlanned: the production signing key ceremony

Alongside: keeping watch

Two checks in Quản trị (Admin) tell the team when a platform starts blocking us or a tool breaks, before people notice it link by link. Any alert puts a ⚠ on the Admin link.

  • Captures by platform: for each of the last 7 days, how many captures per platform were blocked (login wall, rate-limited, failed) out of all. It is built from results already recorded, so it costs nothing.
  • Scheduled canary: once a day, a fixed list of known links (a public post that should be complete, a known restricted post…) is captured without storing anything, and each result is compared with what it should be. A worse result raises the alert. This uses a little paid proxy traffic for Threads and Instagram links, about 0.5 MB each per run.
Builthealth.py · upkeep.pyfrom auto-archiver: platform_status canary

Side branch: the Internet Archive

A project can choose “Share with the Internet Archive” (off by default). Submissions there are public and permanent, so the hub never sends anything automatically otherwise. The Wayback copy is an extra, external copy. It is never part of a sealed capture and never changes a verdict. Threads and Instagram are skipped, because the Archive would only store their login page. For Facebook it is sent the public embed address.

Running cost

What the residential proxy costs

Only Threads and Instagram use paid traffic. Facebook, websites and every media file go direct. These figures were measured on 21 October 2026, on the server, through the proxy, with a byte counter in front of it:

CapturePaid traffic before the splitPaid traffic nowResult
Threads, text and image5.8–6.9 MB0.38–0.62 MBcomplete
Threads, text only1.8 MB0.16 MBrestricted (a known restricted post)
Instagram, video post2.25 MB0.59 MBcomplete, with its video
Per save≈ 0.5 MBThreads or Instagram, typical
2,000 saves a month≈ 1 GBincluding about 20% for retries and re-captures
Monthly bill$1–2at $1/GB, pay-as-you-go; unused traffic doesn’t expire

The scheduled canary adds about 0.5 MB per Threads or Instagram link per day, a few cents a month. We target the country (Vietnam), not a city: platforms restrict content by country, and city targeting costs extra. Long threads with many images, and videos on Threads, cost more per save. A monthly traffic limit in the DataImpulse dashboard caps the bill. Prices from DataImpulse’s pricing page; sticky ports from its docs.

Known gaps

Capture

  • Replayable archives for ordinary web pages (built for social posts; web pages are off because recording slowed heavy pages)
  • Logged-in capture for Facebook (decision pending: throwaway accounts, a hosted service, or logged-out only)
  • OCR check of screenshots for walls
  • Treat Threads, Instagram and Facebook as one “Meta”, and run one Meta capture at a time. Today a block on one platform holds back only that platform’s queue, and two captures can hit Meta at once. Since Meta’s limits tend to arrive together (Threads and Instagram limited our server the same day), this would back off sooner and spend less on blocked attempts.
